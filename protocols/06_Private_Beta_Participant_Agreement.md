This document answers the piece that sits underneath all four Phase 3
gap docs: before anyone is let onto the platform, they need to
understand — in plain language, before they agree — what Edin does, what
it isn't, what data it collects, and specifically that certain language
triggers a safety review that isn't private in the way a personal journal
is. Chelsey personally selecting each participant doesn't reduce the need
for this — if anything it raises the bar, since she's vouching for the
platform directly rather than a stranger opting into a public product.

## WHAT THIS COVERS

Two things bundled into one document, because at this beta stage a
participant needs both at once, before they ever open the app:

1. **Informed consent** — what Edin is, what it does with their data,
   and specifically how the crisis-safety layer works
2. **Participant agreement** — the basic terms of being in a private,
   invite-only, experimental beta

## SECTION 1 — WHAT EDIN IS (AND ISN'T)

Pulled directly from `04_DSM5_Jungian_Language_Line.md`'s Scope &
Positioning section, since this is the one thing that has to be
consistent everywhere:

> Edin is a self-tracking and metacognitive training system. It observes
> patterns in data you generate — dreams, biofeedback, behavior, symbolic
> material — and reflects them back to you. Edin does not diagnose,
> treat, or manage any health condition. Edin is not a therapist, not a
> medical provider, and not a replacement for either. If you currently
> have a therapist, Edin does not replace that relationship — it sits
> alongside it. This is a coaching and self-development tool, not a
> clinical one.

---

## SECTION 2 — WHAT DATA EDIN COLLECTS

Plain-language list, mapped to what's actually in the database:

- Dream journal entries you write (full text, tags you assign, your
  self-reported emotional intensity and body sensations)
- Biofeedback data if connected (heart rate variability, sleep data, EEG)
- Behavioral/goal check-ins and whether you followed through on things
- Results from any Genius Constitution assessment you complete
- If applicable: any flagged safety event (see Section 3 — this is the
  one category of data that isn't just yours to keep private)

**What Edin does with it:** looks for patterns across this data over
time and reflects them back to you as insights. It is not sold, not used
to train a public model, and not shared outside this private beta.

## SECTION 3 — THE CRISIS SAFETY LAYER (READ THIS PART CAREFULLY)

This is the part that must be understood and agreed to *before* anyone
uses the platform — it's the difference between real informed consent
and a buried clause.

> Edin is built to watch for a specific, narrow category of language:
> direct statements about suicide, self-harm, or intent to harm someone
> else. This is not general sentiment monitoring — everyday venting,
> frustration, sadness, or dark humor does not trigger anything.
>
> If that specific kind of language does appear, two things happen
> automatically:
>
> 1. **You immediately receive real crisis resources** — the 988
>    Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, the Crisis Text Line, and a reminder to
>    call 911 or go to an emergency room if you're in immediate danger.
> 2. **The event is logged, and Chelsey is notified to review it
>    directly** — this is not a fully private journal in this one
>    respect. Chelsey is not a therapist and does not manage crises
>    clinically; her role at that point is to make sure you're not alone
>    with it and to help you get to real support.
>
> Edin is not a 24/7 monitored crisis service. If you are in immediate
> danger, do not wait for Edin or for Chelsey to respond — call 988 or
> 911 directly.

**Therapist coordination (per-client choice, not assumed):**

> ☐ I have an outside therapist, and I consent to Chelsey sharing a
>   flagged safety event with them directly, so they have full context.
> ☐ I have an outside therapist, but I do NOT consent to this — I
>   understand Chelsey may still reach out to me directly if something is
>   flagged.
> ☐ I do not currently have an outside therapist.

## SECTION 4 — WHAT THIS BETA IS

- This is a **private, invite-only beta**. Chelsey is personally
  selecting each participant; this is not a public product.
- Features, prompts, and how Edin responds may change without notice —
  this is an active work in progress, not a finished product.
- Participation is voluntary. You can stop using Edin and ask for your
  data to be deleted at any time, **except** that a flagged safety event
  already logged and reviewed is retained as a safety record rather than
  deleted on request — this exception exists so a real safety concern
  can't be erased after the fact.
- Edin/The Way of Genius makes no guarantee of uptime, data preservation
  in the event of a technical failure, or any particular outcome from
  using the platform.

## WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT DECIDE

- **Minors.** This draft assumes adult participants. If any current or
  future participant is under 18, this document does not cover that —
  parental/guardian consent, different data handling, and likely
  different legal obligations entirely would apply. Flag this explicitly
  before inviting anyone under 18.
- **Data retention specifics** — how long non-flagged data is kept, and
  the exact process for a deletion request, needs a concrete answer.
- **What happens if a participant is also Chelsey's therapy-adjacent
  client in some other business relationship** — worth naming explicitly
  if that overlap exists for anyone in the cohort, since it could blur
  the "Edin is not therapy" line in practice even when it's clear on
  paper.
- **Governing state/jurisdiction** for the agreement — needs to be named.
- **Liability/indemnification language** — this draft is informed
  consent plus basic terms; a real liability limitation clause is
  exactly the kind of thing that needs an attorney's specific wording,
  not a drafted-by-AI paragraph.

## BEFORE THIS GOES TO ANYONE

This document is strong groundwork, not a finished contract. Given the
subject matter — flagged self-harm data, health-adjacent information,
real clients — get it in front of an attorney once, even briefly, before
the first person signs it. This doesn't require retaining a lawyer
long-term: a flat-fee one-time document review from a small-business or
health-law attorney is the realistic, affordable version of this step.
Many state bar associations run lawyer-referral services specifically
for this kind of bounded, one-time review at a set low cost — that's
worth looking into before this goes further, given no attorney is
currently retained (per this conversation).
